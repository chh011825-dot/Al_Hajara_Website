A Property-Buying Guide for Overseas Pakistanis

AL-HAJARA Estate & Builders · 6 min read

Buying or building property in Pakistan while living abroad comes with a real problem: you can't personally walk the plot, sit across from the seller, or check on the construction crew every week. That gap is exactly where most overseas property problems start — not from bad intentions, but from information that never quite makes it back to the person paying for it.

1. Set up a valid Power of Attorney

If someone will be acting on your behalf in Pakistan — signing documents, collecting possession, or making payments — they need a properly executed Power of Attorney (POA), typically attested through the Pakistani consulate or embassy in your country of residence. Keep this scoped narrowly to what's actually needed; a POA that's too broad hands over more control than intended.

2. Never rely on photos alone

Marketing photos and even "recent" site photos can be old, borrowed, or simply not of the plot or unit you're buying. Before committing funds, ask for a live video call from the actual site — a real-time walk-through lets you ask questions and see the surroundings as they are right now, not as they were when a photo was taken.

A seller or builder who's reluctant to do a live video call before payment is one of the clearest warning signs in an overseas transaction.

3. Route payments properly

Property payments from abroad should go through proper banking channels, with clear documentation tying the transfer to the specific transaction. This isn't just good practice — it also protects your ability to bring proceeds back out of Pakistan later if you resell, since undocumented cash transactions complicate repatriation.

4. Get independent verification, not just the seller's word

Every claim — title, development status, absence of disputes, absence of outstanding dues — should be verified by someone working for you, not by the party selling you the property. This is true whether you're buying a resale plot or paying a builder to construct from scratch. A second, independent set of eyes catches problems before they become expensive.

5. Build a paper trail for construction progress

If you're building rather than buying finished, ask for dated photo and video updates at each construction milestone — foundation, grey structure, roofing, finishing — tied to the payment schedule. This does two things: it keeps the builder accountable to a visible timeline, and it gives you a record if a dispute ever arises later.

6. Plan the handover before you need it

Decide early who will physically receive keys, documents and utility connections on your behalf, and make sure that person is named in your POA specifically for this purpose. Leaving this until the last minute is one of the most common reasons overseas handovers get delayed by months.
